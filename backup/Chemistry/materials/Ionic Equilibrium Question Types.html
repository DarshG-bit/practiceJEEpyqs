3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Solubility product and solubility of a salt in pure water – 16 questions

What is asked: Find the solubility from Ksp, Ksp from the solubility or ion concentrations, or compare salts.

What to master: The relation between Ksp and s for each salt type; molar solubility from g/L; roots such as the seventh root for Zr₃(PO₄)₄.

Forms that appear: PbS, Bi₂S₃, Ca(OH)₂, Zr₃(PO₄)₄, Cr(OH)₃, calcium phosphate in W g per 100 mL, AgCl and CaF₂ from mass solubility, a solubility curve, the order of Ksp of Ca(OH)₂, AgBr, PbS and HgS.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (3), 2021 (3), 2022 (3), 2023 (1), 2024 (2), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

Ksp from solubility, and solubility from Ksp, for salts of any formula (AB, AB₂, A₂B₃, A₃B₄ ...): 10 questions

Ksp from given concentrations, mass solubility or an experimental value: 3 questions

Comparing salts, Ksp order and solubility curves: 3 questions

Type 2: Buffers and pH during partial neutralisation – 15 questions

What is asked: Find the pH of a buffer, the ratio of salt to acid needed, the effect of adding HCl, or the pH when a weak acid or base is partly neutralised.

What to master: Henderson–Hasselbalch for acid and base buffers; mole table after a reaction; half-equivalence point; pKa + pKb = 14.

Forms that appear: acetic acid with sodium acetate, NH₃ with NH₄Cl, benzoic acid with benzoate, 0.05 mol HCl added to a buffer, H₂SO₄ with NH₄OH, NH₄OH with HCl, NaOH with acetic acid.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (2), 2021 (2), 2022 (4), 2023 (3), 2024 (2), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

Buffer pH, buffer preparation and statements about buffers: 7 questions

Buffer after adding a strong acid or base, and the OH⁻ of a base–salt mixture: 3 questions

pH after partial neutralisation of a weak acid or base: 5 questions

Type 3: pH of strong acids and bases: dilution and mixing – 14 questions

What is asked: Find the pH of a strong acid or base, after dilution or after mixing acids and bases.

What to master: pH and pOH; the number of H⁺ or OH⁻ per formula; moles and total volume after mixing; the 1-unit change for a 10-fold dilution.

Forms that appear: Ba(OH)₂ at 298 K, 0.001 M NaOH, HCl diluted with equal volume of water, volume of water to change pH from 1 to 2, HCl with H₂SO₄, NaOH with H₂SO₄ in solutions A and B, Ca(OH)₂ giving pH 12.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (1), 2021 (2), 2022 (2), 2023 (5), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

pH of a single strong acid or base, dilution and pH change: 7 questions

pH after mixing strong acids and bases: 7 questions

Type 4: pH of weak acids, weak bases and salts – 14 questions

What is asked: Find the pH, Ka, Kb or the degree of dissociation of a weak electrolyte, or the pH or nature of a salt solution.

What to master: Ostwald's dilution law; [H⁺] = √(KaC); Kw = Ka·Kb; hydrolysis formulas for the four types of salt; the product of consecutive Ka values.

Forms that appear: butyric acid, ethylamine, HX diluted to pH 6, weak acid in HCl, sulphurous acid, calcium lactate, ammonium phosphate, ammonium acetate, NH₄Cl, CH₃COONa, CO₂ in a soft drink.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (2), 2021 (3), 2022 (2), 2023 (1), 2024 (2), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

pH, degree of dissociation and Ka or Kb of a weak acid or base: 8 questions

pH and nature of salt solutions (hydrolysis): 6 questions

Type 5: Common-ion and pH effect on solubility – 9 questions

What is asked: Find the solubility of a salt in a solution of a common ion, a base or a buffer.

What to master: s = Ksp ÷ [common ion]ⁿ; fixed [OH⁻] in a buffer; the factor 2 in (2s)²; removal of an anion by H⁺.

Forms that appear: CdSO₄ in H₂SO₄, BaSO₄ in K₂SO₄, Zn(OH)₂ and Al(OH)₃ in NaOH, Ag₂CO₃ in AgNO₃, PbI₂ in Pb(NO₃)₂, AgCl in HCl, KCl, water and AgNO₃, AgCN in a pH 3 buffer, Cd(OH)₂ at pH 12.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2021 (4), 2022 (1), 2023 (1)

Which form was asked:

Solubility in a solution with a common ion: 7 questions

Solubility changing with pH (salt of a weak acid, hydroxides in a buffer): 2 questions

Type 6: Precipitation: Qsp against Ksp and selective precipitation – 8 questions

What is asked: Decide whether a precipitate forms on mixing, which of two salts precipitates first, or the pH at which a hydroxide starts to precipitate.

What to master: New concentrations after mixing; Qsp > Ksp; [OH⁻] needed for Mg(OH)₂; the [Ag⁺] needed for AgCl and Ag₂CrO₄.

Forms that appear: AB₂ and XY solutions for AY₂, NH₄OH added to A²⁺ and B³⁺, Ba(NO₃)₂ with NaF, PbCl₂ from Pb(NO₃)₂ and NaCl, Ca(OH)₂ with sodium sulphate, Cl⁻ and CrO₄²⁻ with AgNO₃.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (1), 2021 (1), 2023 (1), 2024 (2), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Will a precipitate form? Qsp compared with Ksp after mixing: 6 questions

pH at which a hydroxide precipitates, and fractional precipitation: 2 questions

Type 7: Titrations, indicators and titration curves – 8 questions

What is asked: Choose correct statements about indicators and titrations, pick the titration curve, or find a concentration from burette readings.

What to master: Colour and pH range of phenolphthalein and methyl orange; the shape of strong and weak acid–base curves; concordant readings.

Forms that appear: methyl orange at the end point, an incorrect statement on indicators, phenolphthalein statements, strong acid with weak base, pH curve of NaOH with HCl, NH₄OH with HCl, Na₂CO₃ with HCl readings.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2021 (2), 2022 (2), 2023 (3)

Which form was asked:

Indicators and their colours, and the end point: 5 questions

Titration curves and titration readings: 3 questions

Type 8: Acid–base concepts: Lewis, Brønsted, amphoteric, Kw – 8 questions

What is asked: Pick the correct statement or species from the Lewis and Brønsted definitions, amphoteric behaviour or the effect of temperature on Kw.

What to master: Definitions of acids and bases; lone pair counts; conjugate pairs; amphoteric hydroxides; Kw and temperature.

Forms that appear: non-bonded electrons in NO₂⁻, which compound cannot act as a Lewis base, an amphoteric hydroxide, amphoteric behaviour of water, strongest Brønsted base, pH of water on heating, pH of 10⁻⁸ M HCl.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (1), 2021 (1), 2022 (1), 2023 (1), 2024 (1), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Lewis and Brønsted acids and bases, amphoteric species: 5 questions

Kw, temperature and statements about pH: 3 questions

6. Suggested study order for students

Start with solubility (Types 1, 5 and 6): 33 questions (35%). Master Ksp = xˣyʸ s^(x+y) and the common-ion step.

Then pH calculations (Types 2, 3 and 4): 43 questions (46%). Learn which formula goes with which type of solution.

Then titrations and acid–base concepts (Types 7 and 8): 16 questions (17%), mostly statements and facts.

7. A routine to follow for every question

Write what is given and what is asked; write the dissociation or neutralisation equation.

Convert every quantity to moles and mol/L (watch mL, g per 100 mL and g/L).

Choose the model: strong acid or base, weak acid or base, salt, buffer (acid + conjugate base), or a saturated solution (Ksp).

Use the formula of that model and keep the coefficient of every ion (the factor 2 in (2s)²).

Check the answer: pH between 0 and 14, s smaller than the common-ion limit, the rounding asked (nearest integer).
