How to read this: a “type” is the formula, law or technique that decides the answer. So every type below tells you what the question looks like and which formula or method to use.

3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Stoichiometry: reactions, limiting reagent and percentage yield – 51 questions

What is asked: Find the mass, moles or volume of a reactant or product from a balanced equation, decide the limiting reagent, or find the percentage yield.

What to master: Balanced equations and mole ratios; converting between grams, moles and litres at STP; limiting reagent from mole ÷ coefficient; percentage yield.

Forms that appear: combustion of methane or butane, 81 g Al with 128 g O₂, 5 mol BaCl₂ with 2 mol Na₃PO₄, aniline to acetanilide, benzene to nitrobenzene, blast-furnace reduction of Fe₃O₄.

Asked in: 2019 (4), 2020 (4), 2021 (9), 2022 (10), 2023 (6), 2024 (9), 2025 (9)

Which form was asked:

Mass, moles or volume of a product or reactant from a balanced equation: 27 questions

Limiting reagent and the amount of product formed: 16 questions

Percentage yield and multi-step reactions: 8 questions

Type 2: Concentration terms: molarity, molality, mole fraction, mass percent, ppm – 40 questions

What is asked: Convert between the concentration terms using density, or find the molarity after dilution, mixing or neutralisation.

What to master: Definitions of M, m, X, w/w % and ppm; the basis method (1 L or 100 g); M₁V₁ + M₂V₂ = M V for mixing; n-factor method in titrations; 11.2 × M for volume strength.

Forms that appear: molality of a 3 M NaCl solution of density 1.25 g mL⁻¹, mole fraction in a 100 molal solution, mass percent of a mixed sugar solution, volume strength of H₂O₂, ppm of iron or magnesium.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (8), 2021 (7), 2022 (7), 2023 (5), 2024 (5), 2025 (5)

Which form was asked:

Converting between molarity, molality, mole fraction and mass percent (density given): 21 questions

Molarity from a given mass, dilution, mixing and titration: 14 questions

ppm and volume strength of hydrogen peroxide: 5 questions

Type 3: Percentage composition and molecular formula – 29 questions

What is asked: Find the percentage of C, H, N or O from combustion data, or the empirical and molecular formula of a compound or a hydrocarbon.

What to master: Mass of C from CO₂ (12/44), mass of H from H₂O (2/18); the mole-ratio method for empirical formula; n = molar mass ÷ empirical formula mass; gas-volume method for hydrocarbons.

Forms that appear: 0.492 g of compound giving CO₂ and H₂O, 85.8% carbon with molar mass 84, composition 54.2% C, 9.2% H, 36.6% O, a hydrocarbon needing 6 volumes of O₂ and giving 4 of CO₂.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (1), 2021 (3), 2022 (6), 2023 (8), 2024 (2), 2025 (7)

Which form was asked:

Percentage composition and empirical / molecular formula (including combustion analysis): 24 questions

Formula of a hydrocarbon from gas volumes or equivalents of oxygen: 5 questions

Type 4: Mole concept: atoms, molecules and molar mass – 14 questions

What is asked: Count moles, atoms or molecules in a given mass or volume, or find a molar mass or average atomic mass.

What to master: n = m/M = V/22.4 = N/N_A; atoms per molecule; average atomic mass from isotopic abundance.

Forms that appear: atoms in 8 g of sodium, molecules in 2.8375 L of O₂ at STP, atoms of N in 681 g of C₇H₅N₃O₆, Fe atoms in haemoglobin, molar mass of A from weights of AB₂ and A₂B₂.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (1), 2021 (2), 2022 (3), 2023 (2), 2024 (1), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

Number of moles, atoms and molecules from mass or gas volume: 12 questions

Average atomic mass and finding a molar mass: 2 questions

Type 5: Significant figures and basic definitions – 5 questions

What is asked: Count the significant figures in a number or in a calculation, or choose the correct statements about mass, weight and volume.

What to master: Rules for significant figures and the rounding of a product or quotient; definitions of the basic quantities.

Forms that appear: 0.00340, 0.00253 versus 1.0003, the answer of (0.02858 × 0.112)/0.5702, statements about weight and mass.

Asked in: 2021 (2), 2022 (1), 2023 (1), 2025 (1)

5. Suggested study order for students

Start with stoichiometry and concentration (Types 1 and 2): 91 questions (65%). Everything in the chapter uses the mole.

Then percentage composition and molecular formula (Type 3): 29 questions (21%).

Then the basic mole counts and significant figures (Types 4 and 5): short, quick marks.

6. A routine to follow for every question

Write the balanced equation (or the definition) and underline what is given and what is asked.

Convert every quantity to moles (mass ÷ molar mass, gas volume ÷ 22.4 at STP, particles ÷ N_A).

Use the mole ratio or the basis method (1 L, 100 g or 1 kg of solvent) to reach the asked quantity.

For two reactants, divide moles by the coefficient to find the limiting reagent before you calculate the product.

Check the unit, the rounding asked (nearest integer) and that the answer is sensible.
