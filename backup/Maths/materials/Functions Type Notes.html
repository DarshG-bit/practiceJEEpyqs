How to use these notes

1. Do these pages BEFORE the practice sheet, one type at a time.

2. Close all books and notes. Fill the blanks from memory; leave a gap if you cannot recall.

3. Then press “Show completed notes” under that type, correct your answers in a different colour (or your own notes), and note what you missed.

4. Now do that type's questions on the sheet. Come back to the notes after the sheet to add anything new.

5. Re-fill the same page a week later with the prompts covered – this is what makes it stick.

Opens the print screen: choose “Save as PDF”. Only the types you have written in are included.
Move my notes to another device (advanced)

The PDF is for reading and printing. To carry your notes to another phone or computer so you can keep editing them, save a notes file here and load it on the other device.

Typing in the notes boxes
  • Your answers are saved on this device only. Use “Save my notes as PDF” to keep a copy. To move your notes to another device, use the advanced option under the buttons.
  • Tap a box and use the bar at the bottom of the screen.
  • Subscript or superscript: select the text and press x₂ or xⁿ, or press the button, type, and press it again.
  • Back to normal text: press Aa, or press the right arrow at the end of the subscript or superscript.
  • Fraction: press a/b, type the top, press Tab, type the bottom, press Tab again to continue.
  • You can also just type 1/2 or sqrt(x).
Keyboard shortcuts (computer keyboard)
  • Ctrl+H: superscript
  • Ctrl+L: subscript
  • Ctrl+Space: back to normal text
  • Ctrl+F: fraction (Tab moves from top to bottom to out)
  • Ctrl+I: integral ∫
  • Ctrl+S: sigma Σ
  • Ctrl+R: square root √
  • Ctrl+P: pi π
  • Ctrl+E: infinity ∞
  • Ctrl+> (Ctrl+Shift+. on most keyboards): arrow →; press again for the “implies” arrow ⇒
  • These work only while you are typing in a notes box. On a phone use the bar at the bottom.

How do I start a functional equation?

Put x=0, y=0 or y=1 to get f(0) and f(1), then look for a pattern in f(n).

How do I use f(x)+kf(1x)?

Write the same equation with x→1x; solve the pair for f(x).

How do I add many values of f?

Show f(x)+f(1−x) is a constant, then pair the first and last terms.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Forgetting f(0) or f(1) when the equation holds only for natural numbers.

• Taking f(n) as an A.P. when the equation is multiplicative.

• Counting the wrong number of pairs in a sum.

3. Concepts and skills

How do I find a repeated composition?

Compute f∘f and f∘f∘f; look for a cycle or a pattern in the constants.

How do I find f from f(g(x))?

Write f in its general form, expand and match coefficients.

How do I find an inverse?

Put y=f(x), solve for x in terms of y, and swap the names.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Writing f(g(x)) as g(f(x)).

• Not checking the domain of the inner function.

• Stopping before the pattern of the composition repeats.

3. Concepts and skills

How do I find the range of a rational function?

Write y=f(x) as a quadratic in x and use the discriminant, remembering the excluded points.

How do I find the range of a trigonometric expression?

Reduce to one function of sinx or cosx and use its bounds.

What about [x] on an interval?

Split the interval where [x] is constant and take the union of the images.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the discriminant without removing the values that correspond to excluded x.

• Writing a closed end where the value is not attained.

• Missing a piece when the function is piecewise.

Many-one

3. Concepts and skills

How do I test one-one?

Find two inputs with the same output, or show f′ keeps one sign.

How do I test onto?

Find the range and compare it with the stated codomain.

What if the codomain is not the range?

The function is into, not onto; the answer follows the codomain given.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Checking the range but not the codomain.

• Calling a function one-one from one pair of values.

• Ignoring the domain restriction.

3. Concepts and skills

How do I solve with a modulus?

Split at the zeros of each modulus and solve in every interval; keep only roots lying in that interval.

How do I solve with [x]?

Put [x]=n, solve for x and check n≤x<n+1 for each integer n.

When do I draw graphs?

When both sides are simple curves; count the points where they cross.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Keeping a root that lies outside its own case.

• Counting a repeated root twice.

• Forgetting that [x] is constant only on [n,n+1).
