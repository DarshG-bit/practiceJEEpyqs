Functional equations and domains (Types 1 and 2) alone are 71 questions (42%). About 22% of the questions ask for a numerical (integer) answer, so there are no options to guess from.

How to read this: a “type” is the formula, law or technique that decides the answer. So every type below tells you what the question looks like and which formula or method to use.

3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Functional equations and identities – 37 questions

What is asked: A relation such as f(x + y) = f(x)f(y), f(x) + 2f(1/x) = …, or f(x) + f(1 − x) = c is given and a value, a sum or a constant is asked.

What to master: Put special values (0, 1), replace x by 1/x or 1 − x to get a second equation, and recognise f(n) as an A.P. or G.P.

Forms that appear: f(x + y) = f(x) f(y) with f(1) = 3 and Σf(k) = 3279; f(x) + 2f(1/x) = x² + 5; Σ f(k/82) with f = 2ˣ/(2ˣ + √2); f(x + y) = f(x) + f(y) − xy.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (4), 2021 (5), 2022 (5), 2023 (11), 2024 (1), 2025 (8)

Which form was asked:

Additive and multiplicative equations f(x + y) = …: 16 questions

Relations of f(x) with f(1/x), f(ax) and f(24/x): 10 questions

Sums of f(k/n) using a pairing f(x) + f(1 − x) = constant: 5 questions

Finding f from given values or an identity: 6 questions

Type 2: Domain of a function – 34 questions

What is asked: The domain of a function built from inverse-trigonometric, logarithmic, radical or greatest-integer pieces is to be found, often as (α, β) with a combination of the ends asked.

What to master: Intersect the conditions: log argument > 0, base > 0 and ≠ 1, radicand ≥ 0, |argument| ≤ 1 for sin⁻¹ and cos⁻¹.

Forms that appear: sin⁻¹((x − 1)/(2x + 3)), cos⁻¹((x² − 4x + 2)/(x² + 3)), log₇(1 − log₄(x² − 9x + 18)), log_e(2x − 3)/(5 + 4x) + sin⁻¹((4 + 3x)/(2 − x)).

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (1), 2021 (8), 2022 (5), 2023 (5), 2024 (6), 2025 (8)

Which form was asked:

Domain with inverse-trigonometric functions: 22 questions

Domain with logarithms, radicals and rational functions: 8 questions

Domain with greatest integer and composite functions: 4 questions

Type 3: Composite and inverse functions – 28 questions

What is asked: A composition f∘g, a repeated composition, or an inverse function is to be evaluated, simplified or used to find unknown constants.

What to master: Compute f(g(x)) step by step; for f∘f∘… look for a cycle; for the inverse swap x and y.

Forms that appear: f(x) = (4x + 3)/(6x − 4) and g = f∘f, then g∘g∘g(4); f = 2x/√(1 + 9x²) composed 10 times; (f∘g)⁻¹ given and f(g(ac)) + g(f(b)).

Asked in: 2019 (5), 2020 (3), 2021 (4), 2022 (6), 2023 (4), 2024 (6)

Type 4: Counting functions – 23 questions

What is asked: The number of functions (one-one, onto, many-one or with a condition such as f(1) + f(2) = f(3)) between finite sets is asked.

What to master: Count by cases on the restricted values; one-one: P(n, r); onto: inclusion-exclusion; all functions: nʳ.

Forms that appear: one-one maps f: A → B with f(1) + f(3) = 14; onto functions f: R → S with f(a) ≠ 1; functions {1,2,3,4} → {1,…,6} with f(1) + f(2) = f(3).

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (1), 2021 (4), 2022 (5), 2023 (5), 2024 (4), 2025 (3)

Type 5: Range of a function – 21 questions

What is asked: The range of a rational, trigonometric, logarithmic or greatest-integer expression is to be found, often as [α, β] with a combination asked.

What to master: Set y = f(x) and apply the discriminant, or use the bounds of sin x, cos x and A.M. ≥ G.M.; split piecewise functions.

Forms that appear: (5 − x)/(x² − 3x + 2); (sin⁴θ + 3cos²θ)/(sin⁴θ + cos²θ); 3^(−x) + 2^(x)-type sums; range of [x]/(1 + x²) on a given interval.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (2), 2021 (1), 2022 (1), 2023 (7), 2024 (5), 2025 (3)

Type 6: One-one, onto and into functions – 16 questions

What is asked: A function is to be classified as one-one, onto, both, or neither, or the codomain making it onto is asked.

What to master: One-one: f(x₁) = f(x₂) ⇒ x₁ = x₂ or the derivative keeps its sign; onto: range = codomain.

Forms that appear: f(x) = (2ˣ − 2⁻ˣ)/(2ˣ + 2⁻ˣ) from (−∞, ∞) to (−∞, 1); f(n) = highest prime factor; (x² + 2x − 15)/(x² − 4x + 9).

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2021 (3), 2022 (2), 2023 (2), 2024 (4), 2025 (2)

Type 7: Equations, number of solutions and greatest integer – 11 questions

What is asked: The number of real roots of an equation with a modulus or a greatest-integer function, or the sum of its solutions, is to be found.

What to master: Split on the modulus or [x] and solve each case; or draw both sides and count the intersections.

Forms that appear: |x||x + 2| − 5|x + 1| − 1 = 0; x² + 3x + 2 = min{x − 3, x + 2}; [eˣ]² + [eˣ + 1] − 3 = 0; x² − 4x + [x] + 3 = x[x].

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2021 (3), 2023 (2), 2024 (4), 2025 (1)

5. Suggested study order for students

Start with functional equations and domains (Types 1 and 2): 71 questions (42%). Master the special-value method and the domain conditions of log and inverse trigonometric functions.

Then composite and inverse functions and counting (Types 3 and 4): 51 questions (30%).

Then range, one-one/onto and equations (Types 5, 6 and 7): 48 questions.

6. A routine to follow for every question

Read what is asked: a domain, a range, a composition, a count, a classification or a value from an equation.

Pick the method: intersect conditions (domain), y = f(x) and discriminant (range), special values (functional equation), cases (counting).

For functional equations, put x = 0, 1 or replace x by 1/x to get a second equation; recognise an A.P. or G.P. for f(n).

Check the answer against the options: test one value of x, or one small case of the count.
