Completed notes – Type 1

1. How do I spot this type?

• The region is bounded by lines and parabolas such as y2=4ax, y=x2, y=mx+c (or a cubic), and no modulus or special function appears.

• The question says larger region, smaller region, or lists three or more conditions such as y≤4, x≥0 together with two curves.

• A tangent (or normal) at a given point is one of the boundaries, together with the curve and the axis or another line.

2. Formulae and laws

Area between two curves (strip in x)

A=∫ab[f(x)−g(x)]dx where f≥g on [a,b]

Area between two curves (strip in y)

A=∫cd[xR(y)−xL(y)]dy

Area under a curve

A=∫abydx for y≥0

Parabola and its chord

Area between y2=4ax and x2=4by is 16ab3

Parabola and a line

Area between y2=4ax and its latus rectum is 8a23

Parabola y=ax2+bx+c and its chord

Area =|a|(β−α)36 where α,β are the roots of the intersection

Tangent at (x1,y1)

y−y1=f′(x1)(x−x1)

Splitting the region

A=∫ac(f−g)dx+∫cb(h−g)dx if the upper curve changes at x=c

3. Concepts and skills

How do I start every question of this type?

Sketch the curves, mark the intersection points by solving the equations together, and shade the region that is asked for.

When should I integrate with respect to y?

When the curves are given as x=g(y), or when strips parallel to the x-axis give one integral instead of two.

What does 'larger region' mean?

A line or a curve divides the bounded region into two parts; the larger part is the one with the larger area, so find both or subtract the smaller from the total.

How do I use the tangent?

Find the point of contact, differentiate to get the slope, write the equation of the tangent, and then treat it as an ordinary boundary line.

How do I handle three bounding curves?

Find the points where the upper curve (or the lower curve) changes, and add one integral for each piece.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Taking the wrong curve as the upper curve, which gives a negative area.

• Missing an intersection point, or using one that lies outside the region.

• Integrating over the whole interval without splitting where the upper curve changes.

• Using ydx for a region that lies below the x-axis without taking the modulus.

Completed notes – Type 2

1. How do I spot this type?

• The boundary contains |x|, |x−1| or |f(x)|, or the region is given by inequalities with a modulus such as |x|+|y|≤2.

• The curve is y=min{f(x),g(x)} or y=max{f(x),g(x)}.

• The function is defined in cases, or contains [x], and the area under it is asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Modulus

|f(x)|=f(x) if f(x)≥0 and −f(x) if f(x)<0

Area under a modulus curve

A=∫ab|f(x)|dx=∫acfdx−∫cbfdx if f≥0 on [a,c] and f≤0 on [c,b]

Symmetry

If the region is symmetric about the y-axis, A=2∫0a(…)dx

Minimum of two functions

min{f,g}=f where f≤g and g where g≤f

Maximum of two functions

max{f,g}=f where f≥g and g where g≥f

Greatest integer on a unit interval

[x]=n for n≤x<n+1, so the area under [x] over [n,n+1) is n

Region |x|+|y|≤a

A square with area 2a2

3. Concepts and skills

How do I remove a modulus?

Solve f(x)=0 to find the sign changes, and write |f| as f or −f on each piece. For a graph, reflect the negative part about the x-axis.

How do I find where min or max switches?

Solve f(x)=g(x); between two successive roots one function is always the smaller.

How do I find the area when [x] appears?

Write the function as a constant on each unit interval, draw the steps, and add the rectangles.

Can symmetry help?

Yes. If the region is symmetric about the y-axis or the x-axis, find the area of one half and double it.

Why must I draw the graph first?

The graph shows which expression is the boundary on each interval and which intersection points are real.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Removing the modulus without splitting the interval at the sign change.

• Forgetting that ∫abf is negative for f<0, so the area needs |f|.

• Using one expression of a min or max over the whole interval.

• Missing a piece of a case-wise function when the cases overlap the interval only partly.

Completed notes – Type 3

1. How do I spot this type?

• The region is inside or outside a circle such as x2+y2=a2 and is cut by a parabola or a line.

• A circle is cut by a line, and the larger or the smaller part is asked.

• An ellipse x2a2+y2b2=1 or a hyperbola is combined with a line.

2. Formulae and laws

Circle

Area =πr2; the area of a quadrant is πr24

Area under a circle

∫a2−x2dx=x2a2−x2+a22sin−1xa+C

Ellipse

Area of x2a2+y2b2=1 is πab

Part of an ellipse

y=baa2−x2 so A=ba∫a2−x2dx

Hyperbola

∫x2−a2dx=x2x2−a2−a22ln|x+x2−a2|+C

Outside a curve

Area outside = area of the figure − area inside

Sector of a circle

12r2θ

3. Concepts and skills

How do I find the area of a circle cut by a parabola?

Solve the two equations to get the intersection points, integrate the difference over the common range, and double it if the region is symmetric about the x-axis.

When can I avoid integrating?

When the region is a sector, a segment, a triangle or a part of an ellipse whose area is known from πr2 or πab.

How is the larger part found?

Find the smaller part (a segment) and subtract from the area of the circle.

What if the circle is not centred at the origin?

Shift the origin to the centre with X=x−h, Y=y−k; the areas do not change.

How do I handle an ellipse in the first quadrant?

Write y in terms of x, integrate from 0 to the required value, and use sin−1 for the standard integral.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the wrong sign of the square root and getting the lower half of the circle.

• Using the wrong value of sin−1 at the end points (for example sin−11=π2 and sin−112=π6).

• Taking a segment where a sector is asked, or the reverse.

• Using πab for only a part of the ellipse.

Completed notes – Type 4

1. How do I spot this type?

• The area is given as a number or as an expression and a constant such as a, k or λ is to be found.

• A line y=mx or y=α divides, or bisects, the region, or the areas of two regions are in a given ratio.

• The question asks for the largest rectangle in a region, or the value of a constant for which the area is least.

2. Formulae and laws

Area as a function of a parameter

A(a)=∫0af(x)dx, then set A(a)= the given value

Bisecting a region

The dividing line gives half: A1=12A

Areas in a ratio

If A1:A2=m:n then A1=mm+nA

Maximum or minimum

Set dAdk=0 and check d2Adk2

Area of a rectangle in a region

A=base×height, with the height from the curve at the corner

Intersection depends on the parameter

Solve f(x)=g(x) in terms of the parameter before integrating

3. Concepts and skills

How do I find a parameter from an area?

Find the intersection points in terms of the parameter, write the area in terms of the parameter, and equate it with the given value.

How do I use 'bisects the area'?

Find the total area first, then write the area on one side of the line in terms of its constant and equate it to half the total.

Which roots do I reject?

Reject the values that give a negative or zero area, or make the intersection points fall outside the region (check the given range of the parameter).

How are the largest rectangle problems solved?

Put one corner on the curve, write the area of the rectangle in terms of one variable, and differentiate.

How is 'equal areas' used?

Write both areas in terms of the unknown and equate them.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Ignoring the condition on the parameter (for example λ>0) and keeping a negative root.

• Using the whole area instead of half for a bisecting line.

• Not checking that the dividing line actually cuts the region.

• Forgetting to check that a stationary point is a maximum or a minimum.

1. How do I spot this type?

• The boundaries are y=sinx, y=cosx or a combination, with x=0 or x=π2.

• The curves are ex, ax or lnx with a line, or the y-axis as a boundary.

• The curve is of the type xy=c, x(1+y2)=1, x2/3+y2/3=1 or y2=x2(…), and symmetry is needed.

Sine and cosine

x=π4, 5π4 in [0,2π]

Area of one arch of sinx

Exponential

∫exdx=ex, ∫axdx=axlna

Logarithm

3. Concepts and skills

How do I find where sinx and cosx meet?

Solve tanx=1; take the roots that lie inside the given range and split the integral at them.

How do I find the intersection of 2x with a line?

Look for integer values (often x=0 and x=1) and check them in both equations.

What if the curve is not a function of x?

Write x in terms of y (for example x=11+y2) and integrate with respect to y.

How does symmetry help for special curves?

The astroid and curves like y2=x2(…) are symmetric about the axes, so find the area of the first quadrant or one loop and multiply.

How do I integrate 11+y2?

It is tan−1y; combine with the other boundary and evaluate between the intersection values.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Mixing up which of sinx and cosx is larger on an interval.

• Taking only one intersection point when two lie in the range.

• Using lnx integrals with the wrong constant (xlnx−x, not xlnx).

• Forgetting to multiply by the number of symmetric parts.

Completed notes – Type 6

1. How do I spot this type?

• A curve y=y(x) in the first quadrant is described only by a relation between areas, or by a condition on its tangents.

• A differential equation such as dydx=2(x+1) or dydx+x+ay−2=0 gives the curve.

• A functional equation f(x+y)=f(x)f(y) with f′(0) given decides the function whose area is asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Area condition

If ∫0xydt=g(x) then y=g′(x)

Differentiating an integral

ddx∫axydt=y

Separation of variables

dydx=f(x)g(y)⇒∫dyg(y)=∫f(x)dx

Functional equation

f(x+y)=f(x)f(y) and f′(0)=k⇒f(x)=ekx

Area of the curve found

A=∫abydx with the y found

3. Concepts and skills

How do I turn an area condition into an equation?

Write the area as an integral of y with a variable upper limit, set it equal to the given expression, and differentiate both sides.

What do I do after finding y?

Use the given point to fix the constant, sketch the curve, and find the required area as for any other region.

How is f(x+y)=f(x)f(y) used?

Differentiate with respect to y and put y=0 to get f′(x)=f′(0)f(x), hence f(x)=ekx.

How do I read a closed curve from a differential equation?

Integrate to get the equation of the curve (a circle or an ellipse); the area then follows from the standard formulas.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Forgetting the constant of integration or not using the given point.

• Differentiating the area condition but keeping the old limits.

• Taking the curve as a function of x when it is closed.

• Making a sign error when separating the variables.
