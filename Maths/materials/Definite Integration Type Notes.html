Completed notes – Type 1

1. How do I spot this type?

• The limits are 0 and a (or a and b) and the integrand looks like f(x)f(x)+f(a−x), or has x multiplying a function of sinx or cosx.

• The limits are symmetric, −a to a, and the integrand has 11+ex, 11+3x, 11+πx or is clearly even or odd.

• The function repeats (|sinx|, {x}, f(x)+f(x+k)=n) and the upper limit is a large multiple of the period.

2. Formulae and laws

King's rule

∫abf(x)dx=∫abf(a+b−x)dx

Special case 0 to a

∫0af(x)dx=∫0af(a−x)dx

Quotient form

∫abf(x)f(x)+f(a+b−x)dx=b−a2

Even function

∫−aaf(x)dx=2∫0af(x)dx if f(−x)=f(x)

Odd function

∫−aaf(x)dx=0 if f(−x)=−f(x)

The 1/(1+ax) trick

∫−aaf(x)1+exdx=∫0af(x)dx if f is even

Periodic function, period T

∫0nTf(x)dx=n∫0Tf(x)dx; ∫aa+nTf=n∫0Tf

Trigonometric form of King's rule

∫0πxf(sinx)dx=π2∫0πf(sinx)dx

Double-angle form

∫02af(x)dx=∫0a[f(x)+f(2a−x)]dx

3. Concepts and skills

How do I know King's rule will work?

After replacing x by a+b−x the integrand becomes a simple relative of the original, so that adding the two forms gives a constant or a simpler integrand. Try it whenever the limits add up to a nice number such as π, π2 or a.

What is the first thing to check when the limits are −a and a?

Whether f(−x)=f(x) or f(−x)=−f(x); if the function is neither, split it into an even part and an odd part.

Why does 11+ex with symmetric limits become simple?

Because 11+ex+11+e−x=1, adding the integral to itself with x→−x removes the denominator.

How are periodic functions handled?

Find the period, write the interval as complete periods plus a remainder, and use the integral over one period for each complete period.

What does ∫0πxf(sinx)dx reduce to?

π2∫0πf(sinx)dx, so the factor x disappears.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using f(−x)=f(x) for a function that is not even, for example forgetting that xsinx is even but x2sinx is odd.

• Writing 2I=… and then forgetting to divide by 2 at the end.

• Applying a−x instead of a+b−x when the lower limit is not 0.

• Counting the number of periods wrongly when the interval does not start at 0.

Completed notes – Type 2

1. How do I spot this type?

• The integrand contains [x], [x2], [sinx] or {x} and the limits are given numbers.

• The integrand has a modulus such as |x2−3x+2|, |sin2x| or ||x−1|−x|.

• A function is defined as the maximum or minimum of several expressions, or as a case-wise function, and its integral is asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Splitting at break points

∫abf(x)dx=∫acf(x)dx+∫cbf(x)dx

Greatest integer on [n,n+1)

[x]=n for n≤x<n+1, so ∫nn+1[x]dx=n

Fractional part

{x}=x−[x], period 1: ∫0ng({x})dx=n∫01g(x)dx

Modulus

|f(x)|=f(x) if f(x)≥0 and −f(x) if f(x)<0

Modulus and sign of the integral

|∫abf|≤∫ab|f|

[x] with a shift

∫ab[x+k]dx for integer k: shift the limits, ∫a+kb+k[x]dx

[x] and symmetry

[x]+[−x]=−1 if x∉Z and 0 if x∈Z

Maximum and minimum

max{f,g}=f where f≥g; compare the expressions to find the switch points

3. Concepts and skills

How do I integrate [g(x)] over an interval?

Find the points where g(x) crosses an integer; between two such points [g(x)] is a constant, so the integral is that constant times the length of the piece.

How do I integrate |f(x)|?

Solve f(x)=0 in the interval, find the sign of f on each piece, and integrate ±f piece by piece. Use symmetry first if the function is even.

What if [g(x)] has a square, like [x2]?

The break points are at x=k for integers k; write the integral as a sum of lengths such as k+1−k.

How do I handle max and min problems?

Draw the two graphs or solve f(x)=g(x) to find where they cross, and use the larger (or smaller) one on each side.

Why does {x} make an integral easy?

It is periodic with period 1, so only one period has to be integrated.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Forgetting a break point inside the interval, especially for [x2] and |x2−…|.

• Using [x]=x for non-integers, or [−x]=−[x] (it is −[x]−1).

• Taking |x| as x on a negative interval.

• Missing a case where the expression is zero at an end point, which changes the count of intervals.

Completed notes – Type 3

1. How do I spot this type?

• A rational function, a square root of a quadratic, or a difference of two square roots appears and no symmetry is visible.

• A trigonometric rational function such as 13+2sinx+cosx or sinx+cosx9+16sin2x is given.

• The integrand has the form ex(f+f′), xtan−1x, logx or xx(2+logx).

3. Concepts and skills

How do I choose between partial fractions and substitution?

If the denominator factorises into distinct linear or quadratic factors, use partial fractions; if the numerator is the derivative of part of the denominator, use substitution.

What do I do with 1x+a+x+b?

Multiply and divide by the conjugate to get a difference of square roots, which integrates term by term.

How are limits changed after a substitution?

Put the old limits into the substitution to find the new ones; then no back-substitution is needed.

When is the tanx2 substitution the right one?

For integrals of 1a+bsinx+ccosx with no easier route.

How do I spot ex(f+f′)?

The integrand has ex multiplying a sum of a function and its derivative, for example x−1x2ex.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Forgetting to change the limits when the substitution is made.

• Dropping the constant in a partial-fraction split or a sign in a rationalised denominator.

• Using tan−1 formulas without the factor 1a.

• Using the tanx2 substitution over an interval that includes x=π, where tanx2 is not defined.

Completed notes – Type 4

1. How do I spot this type?

• A function is defined as f(x)=∫0xg(t)dt and its maximum, minimum, range or derivative is asked.

• A limit such as limx→0∫0x…x3 has the form 00.

• An equation like f(x)=x+∫0π/2sin(x+y)f(y)dy or ∫0xtf(t)dt=x2f(x) has to be solved for f.

2. Formulae and laws

Fundamental theorem

ddx∫axf(t)dt=f(x)

Leibniz rule

ddx∫a(x)b(x)f(t)dt=f(b(x))b′(x)−f(a(x))a′(x)

Limit of the form 00

limx→a∫axf(t)dt(x−a)n: use L'Hôpital with Leibniz

Integral as a constant

∫01f(t)dt=k is a number; replace it by k and solve for k

Integral with x inside

∫0x(x−t)f′(t)dt=∫0xf(t)dt−…; differentiate twice

Critical points of F(x)=∫axf

F′(x)=f(x); F has a maximum where f changes from + to −

Value at the lower limit

Put x=a in f(x)=…+∫ax… to get f(a)

Differentiate under the integral sign

ddx∫abf(x,t)dt=∫ab∂f∂xdt

3. Concepts and skills

What do I do first with an equation that has ∫0xf(t)dt?

Differentiate both sides once (Leibniz), and find an initial value by putting x=0 in the original equation.

How do I find the number of maxima and minima of F(x)=∫0x2g(t)dt?

F′(x)=2xg(x2); find where it changes sign and count the sign changes of x⋅g(x2).

What if the integral has a constant limit, like ∫0π/2sin(x+y)f(y)dy?

Expand sin(x+y)=sinxcosy+cosxsiny; the integrals of cosyf(y) and sinyf(y) are constants.

How is lim∫0xfxn evaluated?

Differentiate the numerator (Leibniz) and the denominator, and repeat until the limit is no longer of the form 00.

Why is x inside the integrand a problem?

x can be taken out of the integral (it is a constant for t) and the rule is applied to the remaining integral.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Forgetting the chain-rule factor b′(x) when the upper limit is x2.

• Differentiating an integral with x inside the integrand without separating x first.

• Missing the condition obtained by putting x=0 or x=1 in the original equation.

• Treating ∫01f(t)dt as a function of x instead of a constant.

Completed notes – Type 5

1. How do I spot this type?

• A limit of a sum such as limn→∞∑k=1nnn2+k2 with n terms, and the sum is asked.

• The terms are written as f(0)+f(5n)+… or a list of fractions ending with 12n.

• A product limit lim(Un)p/n2, solved by taking logarithms.

2. Formulae and laws

Limit of a sum

limn→∞1n∑r=0n−1f(rn)=∫01f(x)dx

General form

limn→∞b−an∑r=1nf(a+r(b−a)n)=∫abf(x)dx

Limits of integration

Lower limit =limfirst rn; upper limit =limlast rn

Sum of powers

limn→∞1nk+1∑r=1nrk=1k+1

Product limit

lnUn=∑arln(1+r2n2); divide by n or n2, then change the sum to an integral

Standard integrals needed

∫01dx1+x2=π4; ∫01dx1+x=ln2; ∫01xkdx=1k+1

3. Concepts and skills

How do I identify the function f?

Divide every term by n or by n2 so that each term is a function of rn; then replace rn by x.

How do I choose the limits?

Put the smallest r and the largest r into rn and take n→∞; for r=1 to n the limits are 0 and 1.

What if the sum has 2n terms?

The upper limit becomes lim2nn=2, or rescale so that the variable runs over [0,1].

How do I deal with a product?

Take the natural logarithm to turn the product into a sum, evaluate the limit of the sum as an integral, and then take the exponential.

When is a term like n3(n2+k2)(n2+3k2) in the right form?

After dividing numerator and denominator by n4 it is 1n⋅1(1+k2n2)(1+3k2n2).

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Forgetting to divide by n so that the factor 1n (the dx) is missing.

• Taking the limits as 1 to n instead of 0 to 1.

• Putting the upper limit as 1 when the last term is f(2nn).

• Mixing up the starting value of r, which changes the lower limit.

3. Concepts and skills

How do I get a reduction formula?

Integrate by parts, taking the power as the first function, and match the remaining integral with In−1 or In−2.

How do I use the Beta function for ∫01(1−x10)20dx?

Substitute x10=t to get 110∫01t−910(1−t)20dt=110B(110,21).

How do I show terms are in A.P.?

Find a recurrence for In and compute the differences of consecutive terms.

What are the quick checks for a×β(b,c)?

Compare the exponents with m−1 and n−1 to get m and n, and put the factor outside into a.

When do I use B(m,n)=B(m+1,n)+B(m,n+1)?

When two Beta integrals with neighbouring parameters are added, as in I(9,14)+I(10,13).

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Taking B(m,n) with exponents m and n instead of m−1 and n−1.

• Missing the factor 1k after the substitution xk=t.

• Stopping the recurrence one step early (using In−1 instead of In−2).

• Assuming a sequence is an A.P. without computing the common difference.

Completed notes – Type 7

1. How do I spot this type?

• No formula is given for f; only a relation such as f(x)=f(a−x), f(x)+f(a−x)=4 or f(a+b+1−x)=f(x) is known.

• A question asks between which numbers the value of an integral lies, such as 18<I2<14.

• An expression such as ∫12dx2x3−9x2+12x+4 is to be estimated without evaluating it.

2. Formulae and laws

Use of symmetry

If f(x)=f(a−x) and g(x)+g(a−x)=4 then ∫0afg=2∫0af

Shifting the limits

∫abf(x)dx=∫a+cb+cf(x−c)dx

Bounds

If m≤f(x)≤M on [a,b] then m(b−a)≤∫abf≤M(b−a)

Comparison

If f(x)≤g(x) on [a,b] then ∫abf≤∫abg

Extreme values of the integrand

Set ϕ′(x)=0 and compare the values at the critical points and the end points

Mean-value form

∫abf(x)dx=f(c)(b−a) for some c∈(a,b)

Cauchy–Schwarz

(∫abfg)2≤∫abf2∫abg2

3. Concepts and skills

What do I do when f is not given?

Use the functional equation to pair f(x) with f(a−x) or f(x+k) so that the unknown parts cancel or add to a constant.

How are bounds for an integral found?

Find the minimum and the maximum of the integrand on the interval by differentiating, then multiply by the length of the interval.

How do I choose between the options in an inequality question?

Find m and M for the integrand and bound I, then square or take the reciprocal as the options require.

What if the function is positive but not monotonic?

Check the critical points inside the interval, not only the end points.

When can I say a definite integral is zero?

When the integrand is odd on a symmetric interval, or the function and the interval satisfy a sign-reversing symmetry.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using only the end-point values as m and M for a function that has a turning point inside the interval.

• Squaring an inequality that includes negative values.

• Assuming f is even or odd without a given condition.

• Forgetting to double the integral after pairing f(x) with f(a−x).
