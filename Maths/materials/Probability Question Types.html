JEE Main Maths – Probability

Types of questions asked in JEE Main 2019–2025 (176 questions)

Key message: these 176 questions fall into only 7 repeating types. The three biggest types (Types 1, 2, 3) account for 103 questions (59%). Counting-based probability (Type 1) alone is 46 questions (26%). About 16% of the questions ask for a numerical (integer) answer, so there are no options to guess from.

How to read this: a “type” is the formula, law or technique that decides the answer. So every type below tells you what the question looks like and which formula or method to use.

3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Counting-based probability: digits, words, objects, dice, functions and subsets – 46 questions

What is asked: A random selection, arrangement or throw is described and the probability is favourable cases divided by total cases.

What to master: Permutations and combinations; inclusion–exclusion; counting by the last digit, the digit sum or the position; sum tables of two dice; counting one-one and onto functions and subsets.

Forms that appear: a 2 × 2 0–1 matrix is invertible, a seven-digit number is divisible by 2, two squares of a chessboard share a side, 10 balls in 4 boxes, a biased die with probabilities 1/n, a randomly chosen one-one function.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (4), 2021 (11), 2022 (9), 2023 (5), 2024 (8), 2025 (7)

Which form was asked:

Digits, words and arrangements (divisibility, derangements, dictionary order): 11 questions

Selecting and distributing objects (balls, committees, squares, letters to addresses): 9 questions

Dice, coins and biased dice: sums and outcomes: 10 questions

Counting functions, subsets, integers and sample spaces: 16 questions

Type 2: Random variable: probability distribution, mean, variance and expected value – 31 questions

What is asked: A random variable X (number of defectives, heads, a game payoff) is defined, and its probabilities, mean, variance or an expression in them is asked.

What to master: ΣP = 1 to find k; E(X) = ΣxP; Var(X) = E(X²) − (E(X))²; hypergeometric and binomial means and variances; expected gain in a game.

Forms that appear: P(X = x) = k(x + 1)3⁻ˣ, a distribution table with unknown k, variance of the number of rotten apples in a draw, expected gain for a doublet, mean number of tosses in a biased-coin game.

Asked in: 2019 (4), 2020 (3), 2021 (3), 2022 (3), 2023 (8), 2024 (4), 2025 (6)

Which form was asked:

Probability distribution tables: find k, the mean and the variance: 10 questions

Draws without replacement: hypergeometric random variables: 7 questions

Expected value and variance from games, dice and repeated tosses: 14 questions

Type 3: Total probability and Bayes' theorem – 26 questions

What is asked: A result is observed and the probability of the cause is asked (which bag, machine or coin), or the probability of a final outcome after a transfer or unknown composition is asked.

What to master: P(A) = ΣP(Eᵢ)P(A|Eᵢ); P(Eᵢ|A) = P(Eᵢ)P(A|Eᵢ)/P(A); equally likely compositions for an unknown bag.

Forms that appear: a bag chosen at random and the ball drawn is white, three machines and a defective bolt, one ball transferred from Bag I to Bag II, a pack of cards with one card missing, a bag of 6 balls with k black.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (1), 2021 (4), 2022 (3), 2023 (5), 2024 (5), 2025 (6)

Which form was asked:

Bayes' theorem with bags, urns, boxes and coins: 9 questions

Bayes' theorem with factories, populations and percentages: 6 questions

Total probability: transfers, a lost card, an unknown composition and multi-stage experiments: 11 questions

Type 4: Events and set relations: union, conditional probability and independence – 22 questions

What is asked: Probabilities of events or their intersections are given (or implied) and a combination of them is asked, or a statement about independence or mutually exclusive events is to be checked.

What to master: P(A ∪ B); P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B)/P(B); complements; P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B) for independent events; inclusion–exclusion for three events.

Forms that appear: P(exactly one of A and B) = 2/5 and P(A ∪ B) = 1/2, P(A|B) and P(B|A) as roots of 12x² − 7x + 1 = 0, three independent events with the probabilities of 'only E₁', 'only E₂', 'only E₃' given.

Asked in: 2019 (5), 2020 (4), 2021 (4), 2022 (4), 2023 (2), 2024 (1), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Formulas with P(A), P(B) and P(A ∩ B): union, exactly one, complements: 10 questions

Conditional probability: P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B)/P(B) by counting: 5 questions

Independent events and statement-based questions: 7 questions

Type 5: Repeated trials: at least one success, until the first success, alternate-turn games – 22 questions

What is asked: An experiment is repeated (shots, tosses, throws) and the probability of a success pattern, or the least number of trials or turns, is asked.

What to master: P(at least one) = 1 − qⁿ; geometric distribution P(X = k) = qᵏ⁻¹p; sum of a G.P. for alternate turns; C(n, k)pᵏqⁿ⁻ᵏ with independent trials.

Forms that appear: minimum number of tosses for at least one head with probability ≥ 0.9, a die thrown until a six: P(X ≥ 5 | X > 2), A wins if he throws a 5 before B throws an 8, at most two of five machines out of service.

Asked in: 2019 (5), 2020 (5), 2021 (5), 2022 (1), 2023 (2), 2024 (3), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

At least one success and the minimum number of trials: 7 questions

Trials until the first success, and alternate-turn games: 7 questions

Independent trials: exactly k or at least k successes: 8 questions

Type 6: Binomial distribution (out of syllabus) – 15 questions

What is asked: A binomial random variable X ∼ B(n, p) is described by its mean, variance or by two equal probabilities; find n, p or a binomial probability. These questions are marked as out of syllabus in the book.

What to master: np and npq; C(n, r)pʳqⁿ⁻ʳ; P(r₁) = P(r₂) ⇒ n = r₁ + r₂ (for p = 1/2); ratio of consecutive terms.

Forms that appear: the sum and product of the mean and variance are 24 and 128, 3P(X = 0) = P(X = 1), P(X = 3) = 5P(X = 4), a fair coin with P(7 heads) = P(9 heads), mean 8 and variance 4.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2021 (3), 2022 (8), 2023 (2)

Type 7: Random coefficients of an equation, system or matrix – 12 questions

What is asked: The coefficients of a quadratic, the right-hand sides of a linear system or the entries of a 2 × 2 matrix are chosen at random (from dice or a set), and the probability of a property (real roots, unique solution, singular) is asked.

What to master: Discriminant b² − 4ac; determinant of the system and the value that makes it zero; ad = bc for a singular matrix; listing the cases by b.

Forms that appear: ax² + bx + c = 0 with a, b, c from {1, …, 6}, x + y + z = 5, x + 2y + 3z = μ, x + 3y + λz = 1 with λ and μ from two dice, a 2 × 2 matrix with entries from the first 10 primes.

Asked in: 2021 (3), 2022 (2), 2023 (3), 2024 (3), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

Random coefficients: quadratic equations (real, equal or distinct roots): 6 questions

Random entries or coefficients: linear systems and matrices: 6 questions

These do not fit a repeating pattern. See each once; they do not need heavy practice.

6. Suggested study order for students

Start with counting-based probability (Type 1): 46 questions (26%). P and C, dice and digit counts decide most of this chapter.

Then random variables (Type 2: 31) and total probability with Bayes (Type 3: 26): 57 questions (32%).

Then events and set relations (Type 4: 22), repeated trials (Type 5: 22) and random coefficients (Type 7: 12). The binomial type (Type 6, 15 questions) is out of syllabus: do it last, if at all.

7. A routine to follow for every question

Decide first what is random: a selection (counting), a throw (list the outcomes), a draw in stages (tree) or a repeated trial (G.P. or binomial).

If something is observed and the cause is asked, write the causes, their probabilities and the likelihoods, and use Bayes.

For "at least one" use 1 − (probability of none), and for "until the first success" use qᵏ⁻¹p.

For a random variable write all values with probabilities, check ΣP = 1, then mean and variance.

Check that your answer is between 0 and 1 and that the options are in lowest terms before choosing.
